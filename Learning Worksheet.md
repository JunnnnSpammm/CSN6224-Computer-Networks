## Week 1

1. In a data communication system, the five components are ***sender***, ***message***, receiver, transmission medium, and protocol.
2. The three key characteristics that determine communication effectiveness are ***delivery***, ***accuracy***, and timeliness.
3. The three data flow types are ***simplex***, half-duplex, and full-duplex.
4. In a network topology, the four basic types are ***mesh***, ***star***, bus, and ring.
5. A network must meet three key criteria: performance, ***reliabilty***, and ***security***.
6. The three categories of networks based on size and coverage are ***LAN***, ***MAN***, and ***WAN***.
7. In the OSI model, there are ***7*** layers, while in the TCP/IP model, there are typically ***5*** layers.
8. A protocol defines the rules of communication, including syntax, semantics, and ***timing***.
9. The two types of standards are ***de facto*** (adopted by common use) and ***de jure*** (approved by official bodies).
10. In encapsulation, data at the transport layer is called a ***segment (TCP) or user datagram (UDP)***, at the network layer a ***datagram***, and at the data link layer a ***frame***.

## Week 2

1. Analog data refers to information that is ***continuous*** whereas digital data refers to information that has ***discrete*** states.
2. A ***periodic*** signal repeats its pattern over time, while a ***nonperiodic*** signal does not exhibit a repeating cycle.
3. A sine wave is defined by three characteristics: amplitude, frequency, and ***phase***
4. Frequency is measured in ***Hertz (Hz*** and is the number of cycles that occur per second.
5. The formula that shows the relationship between period and frequency is f = 1 / ***T***.
6. The bandwidth of a composite signal is calculated as the difference between the ***highest*** and ***lowest*** frequencies.
7. A digital signal's data rate is measured in bits per second (bps) and is referred to as the ***bit rate***.
8. Attenuation refers to the loss of ***energy*** as a signal travels through a medium.
9. The three major causes of transmission impairment are attenuation, distortion, and ***noise***.
10. Latency is made up of four components: propagation time, transmission time, queuing time, and ***processing delay***.

### Useful Equations
1. **Period:** $T = \frac{1}{f}$
2. **Wavelength:** $\lambda = \frac{\text{Light speed}}{\text{Frequency}}$, where Light speed = $3 \times 10^8$
3. **Bandwidth:** $B = f_h - f_l$
4. **Bits per level:** $\text{Number of bits} = \log_2(L)$
5. **Bit Rate:** $\text{Bit rate (bps)} = 2 \times \text{bandwidth} \times \log_2(L)$
6. **Bit length:** $\text{Bit length} = \text{propagation speed} \times \text{bit duration}$
7. **Amplification:**  $\text{dB} = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)$
8. **Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR):** $\text{SNR} = \frac{P_{\text{signal}}}{P_{\text{noise}}}$
9. **Nyquist Rate (Noiseless Channel):** $\text{Bit rate} = 2B \log_2 L$
10. **Shannon Capacity (Noisy Channel):** $C = B \log_2(1 + \text{SNR})$
11. **Throughput:** $\text{Throughput} = \text{Bandwidth} \times \text{Efficiency}$
12. **Latency:** $\text{Latency} = \text{Propagation delay} + \text{Transmission delay} + \text{Queueing delay} + \text{Processing delay}$
